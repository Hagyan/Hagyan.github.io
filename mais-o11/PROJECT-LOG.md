## 2026-09-22 — specification, first conditional routes

- The original [MAIS-O11 statement](source/spec/MAIS-O11.md) and [MAIS-A1 agenda](source/spec/MAIS-A1.tex) were supplied. The question concerns an actual written proof calculus, not semantic equivalence of arbitrary provability formulas. Early work produced the [slow-verifier manuscript](historical/MAIS-O11-certificates/research-notes/MAIS-O11-slow-verifier-construction.tex) and PDF. Its first quantitative account is historical and has since been repaired.
- **FirstPass** formalized a finite-diagonal contradiction, an abstract Löb interface, and a conditional separation statement. **AuditPass** added internal Löb logic and quantitative polynomial-conversion tools. These checked Lean theorems require hypotheses; they are not instantiated for ordinary PA-bin. The first-pass shell builder and ZIP are listed in [ARTIFACTS.md](ARTIFACTS.md).
- A PA-bin audit separated abstract assumptions from the target checker. **TaggedPass** explored a connective-tag guard and concrete tagged syntax. A later audit found that the blanket “all Hilbert axioms preserve the guard” assertion was false for conjunction introduction. That transfer was withdrawn; the corrected chosen-system construction uses a distinct nullary-predicate guard argument.
- The upper-bound and quotation route introduced raw string-fragment grammars, local checker traces, arithmetic quotation, and charged serialization. **QuotationPass** checked these local statements in Lean. Its restricted proof calculus did not establish short PA-bin proofs of the target Bew relation.

## 2026-09-23 — obstruction, envelope, cumulative certificates

- **ObstructionPass** verified a suffix-assembly cost and showed that a tempting hard family can fail: obtaining short boxes for it would eliminate that separation candidate. **EnvelopePass** expressed the negative speedup condition as a sublinear envelope and checked affine numerical consequences. Neither provides an actual PA-bin lower bound.
- The six early Lean projects were gathered in a checksum-protected [cumulative certificate tree](historical/MAIS-O11-certificates/README.md), with a [progress-report TeX source](historical/MAIS-O11-certificates/research-notes/MAIS-O11-progress-report.tex) and PDF. “Certificate” here means checked conditional or local statements, not a final O11 answer.
- Arithmetic access, compact wire, and complete trace checkpoints extended a typed PA-like syntax and serialization. They remained limited to a small Hilbert fragment; literal Enderton PA includes more logical and arithmetic axioms and generalization.

## 2026-09-24 — parser, checker, compression, and shared equality

- An executable compact parser and restricted AX/MP file checker were added, with Lean round trips and soundness against their own reference calculus. Abbreviations in these files stand for closed terms in a prelude; the agenda permits arbitrary strings and interleaved definitions.
- A graph equality table compared shared terms without expanding the large examples. **CompactEquality**, **SharedPrelude**, **SharedConverse**, **SharedOracle**, and related demos connected the table to formula equality, MP, and selected axiom shapes. Theorems prove agreement with the restricted expanding checker. Fallback axiom cases can still expand.
- A padding example gave a numerical Part 1 separation under a weak reading of “efficient,” but **CompositionBarrier** showed that its quadratic terminal fee contradicts constant-cost MP composition. This is an obstruction to presenting that artificial example as the intended result.
- The first internalization audits identified arbitrary propositional tautologies in Enderton's axiom set. A polynomial-time full checker cannot be assumed merely from compact syntax. An arbitrary short reflection proof may contain compressed tautology records.

## 2026-09-25 — shared graph snapshot and adversarial corrections

- **GraphCompiler**, **GraphSubterms**, **InstantiationGraph**, and **GraphFileCheckerInst** strengthened binder-aware shared comparisons and found a substitution witness hidden in an abbreviation graph. **FragmentBalance** began direct computation on raw string grammars. The [shared-graph checkpoint](historical/checkpoint-notes/MAIS-O11-shared-graph-checkpoint.md) aggregates these intermediate accounts.
- A second audit of the uploaded agenda documented the difference between the restricted four-family checker and full Enderton PA, and the missing exact arithmetic predicate. **SpecTransfer** checked an abstract result: superlinear overhead in the same system, plus finite maxima and bounded-fiber hypotheses, yields increasingly strong Part 1 gaps. It did not prove that PA-bin overhead is superlinear.
- The ordinary constructor-DAG route failed for arbitrary string fragments: a short fragment grammar can expand to a unary spine of exponential depth, forcing an exponential ordinary term DAG. **GraphDepthBarrier** and later **FragmentFamily** formalized the relevant representation facts in Lean. This says nothing by itself about PA-bin proof lengths.
- A separate audit constructed an extensionally correct Δ₁ predicate that lacks Löb's rule, showing why a fixed intensional Bew bridge matters. The tautology and padding notes separated complexity or composition obstructions from any negative result for O11.

## 2026-09-26 — compressed navigation and finite arithmetic experiments

- **GrammarLengthBound**, cached **FragmentBalance**, **FragmentPrefix**, and **FragmentRange** supplied expanded-length, balance, prefix, and interval facts for arbitrary fragment grammars. The earlier idea of obtaining interval minimum by subtracting prefix minima was ruled out; direct traversal repairs that local issue. A Python navigation audit checked examples, including a huge expanded word. Its complexity counts were mathematical/instrumented, not a uniform PA certificate.
- Experimental Python proof objects derived local binary additions and existential Join statements in a PA-admissible calculus. A saved 204-production grammar with 407 local joins was replayed. **SummaryFiniteCheck.lean** independently checks the one finite instance in Lean; it does not replay the Python proof graph or produce the uniform PA-bin acceptance theorem.
- **CodedScan** and notes explored existential expansion tables, polynomial-bit auxiliary codes, and a mathematical PA induction plan. The packed-table antecedent, exact Enderton proof printer, and fixed Bew bridge remained open. These were exploration, not substantive Part 2 proofs.

## 2026-09-27 — focus shifts and chosen-system audit

- The Part 2 roadmap narrowed from “internalize every proof” to the proof of D_P assembled by the standard Löb conversion from a particular input π of □P → P. A polynomial internalization of that assembled proof, plus a polynomial diagonal ledger, would give the positive Part 2 answer. No such internalizer exists yet.
- The compressed-tautology circuit note proposed a proof-producing symbolic evaluator for arbitrary valid string-compressed tautologies. A polynomial-time evaluator for one assignment does not supply a uniform PA correctness proof or a polynomial serialized proof of checker acceptance.
- The chosen-system Part 1 audit separated the slow guarded verifier from the connective-tag construction and ordinary PA-bin. It withdrew a cutoff-n/linear-translation estimate for arbitrary fragments. A robust replacement used cutoff 2^n and a coarse 2^(C(m+1)^2) size ledger, still conditional on concrete checker, fixed-point, guard, and PA derivations.
